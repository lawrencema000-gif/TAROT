The Jungian Shadow Tarot: Why This Spread Works Shadow, in Jungian terms, is the disowned self—traits exiled to keep you safe. Tarot makes the invisible visible by mapping inner patterns onto archetypes you can name and navigate. With clear positions, you move from raw emotion to responsible action. The 7-Card Shadow Spread: Positions and Prompts Lay the cards in a gentle arc or vertical ladder. Pull intentionally; breathe once between each card. Card 1 — The Mask: The persona you use to appear “fine.” What you perform. Card 2 — The Projection: What you judge in others that belongs to you. Card 3 — The Wound: The origin pain shaping defense and desire. Card 4 — The Coping Pattern: How you protect the wound (helpful and harmful). Card 5 — The Gift: Strength and talent trapped in the shadow. Card 6 — The Integration Practice: Concrete behavior that reunites you with the gift. Card 7 — The Outcome of Integration: The life texture that emerges when you act. H3 prompts for journaling after each draw: The Mask: “When do I overperform this role? Who benefits? What costs me?” Projection: “Who triggers me and why? Name the mirrored trait.” Wound: “What memory or belief flares here? What was I needing?” Coping Pattern: “How does this keep me safe? How does it keep me small?” Gift: “Where has this gift already leaked through? What lights in my body?” Integration Practice: “What is one repeatable behavior in seven words or fewer?” Outcome: “If I did this for 30 days, what would change?” If you prefer guided draws with position-by-position coaching, run this spread in Arcana tarot readings with notes saved to your profile for pattern-tracking. Interpreting the Cards: From Insight to Action Read each card first in its position’s language. The Tower as Mask isn’t “disaster,” it’s “I look powerful by never showing fear.” The same card as Wound says, “My safety was shattered; I brace for collapse.” Context is the compass. Majors often point to life-long themes; courts speak to roles you play; minors detail everyday mechanics. Reversals can mark repression, overuse, or a blocked expression of the card’s energy—especially potent in shadow work. Track resonance in your body. Shadow truths sting, then steady. If a card flatlines emotionally, you may be staying at the surface. To avoid story loops, convert every insight into a micro-practice tied to Card 6.
